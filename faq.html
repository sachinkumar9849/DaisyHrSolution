02 — Section

Documents, Cost & Process

A Nepali passport valid for at least six months, your citizenship certificate, passport-size photographs on a white background, and any trade certificates or experience letters you hold. Technical certificates matter — they directly affect the grade and salary an employer will offer you.
For most job orders we accept, the employer bears the visa, air ticket and recruitment cost. Where a service charge is legally payable by the worker, it stays within the ceiling fixed by the Government of Nepal and you receive an official numbered receipt. If anyone asks you for money outside our office counter, report it to us immediately.
Usually 30 to 60 days from selection to departure. The medical takes three to seven days, visa stamping depends on the destination embassy, and the labour permit is normally issued within a week of a complete file. Festival periods and destination-country holidays can extend this.
A medically unfit result is usually specific to one condition, and in many cases it can be treated and re-tested. We tell you exactly what the report said, arrange a re-medical where the condition allows it, and do not charge you again for our own service if the file has to be restarted.
Yes. Every worker leaving Nepal for foreign employment must complete the approved orientation programme and hold the certificate before a labour permit is issued. We book your class at a registered training institute and confirm the schedule with you in advance.
Always. You receive a copy of the contract showing the employer, job title, basic salary, overtime terms, contract period, accommodation and food arrangements — and we go through it with you in Nepali before you sign anything.
03 — Section

After Departure

You leave with the name and direct number of a support contact in our Kathmandu office. That person keeps your contract on file and takes up problems with the employer directly — salary delays, roster changes, accommodation issues or documentation renewals.
Contact our support desk first. The contract you signed is on file here and we raise the discrepancy with the employer. If it is not resolved, we help you file a complaint with the Department of Foreign Employment and, where applicable, claim through the Foreign Employment Welfare Fund and your insurance policy.
Yes. Foreign employment insurance is mandatory and is arranged before your labour permit is issued. You are also registered with the Foreign Employment Welfare Fund, which supports workers and their families in cases of serious illness, injury, death or emergency repatriation.
We assist returning workers with contract renewals and with new placements when a contract ends. Changing employers while abroad is governed by the destination country’s labour law, so speak to us before signing anything or leaving your current workplace.
